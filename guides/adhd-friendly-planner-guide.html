What makes a planner ADHD-friendly?

Plenty of planners are beautiful. The ones that survive contact with an ADHD brain share a few unglamorous design choices.

Undated pages

A dated planner punishes every skipped day with a guilt trail of empty pages, and abandoning it feels like failing. Undated pages make skipping free: pick the planner back up on any day with zero wasted pages and zero shame. This one choice is why many people's fifth planner works where the first four didn't.

One day per page, not one week

Week-per-spread layouts ask you to plan seven days at once — exactly the kind of open-ended horizon that invites overwhelm. A single page with room for three priorities, a time-anchored plan, and a small "actually did" space keeps the horizon at one day.

A brain dump that isn't a to-do list

Working memory is the bottleneck. A dedicated dump page — where everything circling in your head gets written down without being committed to — frees attention for the three things that matter today. Sorting can happen later, or never.

Low-stakes habit tracking

A habit tracker helps until a broken streak turns it into evidence against yourself. Trackers that work treat each day as independent: filled circles accumulate, empty ones carry no penalty and no streak to break.
