How to set your freelance rate (with the math)

Most freelancers guess a rate by copying what others charge. Guessing usually undercharges, because it ignores three things employees never see: non-billable time, business costs, and self-paid taxes.

The formula

Your minimum viable hourly rate is:

(income goal + annual business costs) ÷ (1 − tax set-aside %) ÷ realistic billable hours
  1. Income goal: what you actually want to take home in a year, before personal taxes.
  2. Business costs: software, hardware, insurance, subscriptions, coworking — everything the business pays for.
  3. Tax set-aside: the share of gross revenue you park for taxes. Many freelancers use 25–30% as a planning number; your real rate depends on where you live — ask a tax professional.
  4. Billable hours: the honest number. A 40-hour week is never 40 billable hours. Admin, marketing, proposals, and empty pipeline weeks typically leave 20–25 billable hours in a good week, and most people don't bill 52 weeks a year.

A worked example

Say you want to take home $70,000, spend $4,000/year on the business, set aside 30% for taxes, and realistically bill 23 hours a week for 50 weeks (1,150 hours):

  • Gross needed: (70,000 + 4,000) ÷ 0.70 ≈ $105,700
  • Hourly rate: 105,700 ÷ 1,150 ≈ $92/hour

Notice how far that is from the "$35/hour sounds professional" guess. The gap is taxes and non-billable time, not greed.
