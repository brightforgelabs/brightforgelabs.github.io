Hourly vs day rate vs project pricing

These aren't three philosophies — they're three wrappers around the same base number. Get the base hourly rate right first; then pick the wrapper that fits the work.

Start from one base number

Everything below assumes you know your minimum viable hourly rate — the one derived from your income goal, costs, tax set-aside, and honest billable hours. If you don't have that number, start with our free rate calculator (it runs entirely in your browser) or the rate-setting guide.

Hourly: transparent, but capped

Fits: open-ended work, ongoing retainers billed by time, clients who insist on it.
Trade-off: total transparency for the client, but your income is capped by hours worked, every efficiency gain cuts your pay, and timesheets invite line-item scrutiny.

Day rate: fewer arguments, natural minimum

Fits: workshops, on-site work, intensive sprints.
Trade-off: a day rate (commonly 7–8× your hourly) stops the death-by-15-minute-increments problem and sets a natural minimum engagement. Clients know what a day costs; you stop metering minutes.

Project pricing: highest ceiling, highest risk

Fits: well-defined deliverables you've done before and can estimate confidently.
Trade-off: efficiency finally pays you — but scope creep pays the client unless the boundary is written down. Estimate hours honestly, multiply by your base rate, add a contingency (15–25% is common) for the surprises every project has, and define what's out of scope in the proposal itself — the proposal guide walks through that quote build-up with a worked example.

Project pricing wins when you're fast and the scope holds; hourly protects you when it doesn't. That's the whole decision.
