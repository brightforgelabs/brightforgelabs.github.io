Budgeting and debt payoff in one spreadsheet

Budget apps come and go (and increasingly charge monthly). A spreadsheet you understand is durable: your data, your categories, no subscription. Here's the structure that makes one work.

Four connected tables

  1. Transactions: date, description, category, amount. The raw feed everything else reads from.
  2. Budget: planned amount per category per month, with actuals pulled in by SUMIFS and overspends flagged red. The comparison is the budget — a plan nobody checks against reality is a wish.
  3. Debts: balance, APR, monthly payment per debt. Excel's NPER function turns those three numbers into an estimated payoff date — and can flag the alarming case where a payment doesn't even cover the interest.
  4. Dashboard: savings rate, spending by month, debt overview. One glance, once a week.

The two numbers that change behavior

Most people who stick with tracking report the same two numbers doing the work: the red overspend flag in the week it happens (not a month later), and the estimated debt-free date moving closer when a payment goes up. Both are just formulas — but only if the transactions underneath are current.

Keep the entry cost near zero

Category dropdowns instead of free typing, one row per transaction, and a five-minute weekly session beat any elaborate system you'll abandon in March.
