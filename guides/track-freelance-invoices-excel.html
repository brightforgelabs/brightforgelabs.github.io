How to track freelance invoices in Excel

Unpaid invoices rarely die of malice. They die of forgetfulness — yours. A tracker's whole job is to make "who owes me what, and since when" visible in five seconds.

The three tables you need

  1. Clients: name, contact, default payment terms (net-15, net-30). One row per client.
  2. Projects: client, project name, agreed rate or fee, status. One row per engagement.
  3. Invoices: invoice number, client, project, amount, date sent, due date, date paid. One row per invoice. This is the table that earns its keep.

The formulas that matter

  • Due date: =date_sent + terms_days so it is never typed by hand.
  • Overdue flag: conditional formatting on due_date < TODAY() and date_paid empty. Red rows are the point of the whole system.
  • Outstanding total: =SUMIFS(amount, date_paid, "") — the number to glance at every Monday.
  • Income by month/client: SUMIFS on paid invoices, which quietly becomes your revenue report at tax time.

The habit that makes it work

Update the sheet at the moment you send an invoice — not at the end of the week. A tracker that's 90% current is 0% trustworthy, because you'll double-check everything anyway. Ten seconds per invoice keeps it at 100%.
